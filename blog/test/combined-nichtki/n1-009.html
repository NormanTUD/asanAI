The Higher Structure of Flat Things

Fusion aus: mnist_foam/viewer.html × inftytopoi.html

A flat image and a point of a space are both cross-sections of a hidden higher-dimensional structure. This paper fuses the MNIST Foam Viewer (a 2-D pixel grid revealed as a 3-D point cloud) with the ∞-Topoi Explorer (a point revealed under an unbounded tower of higher morphisms) into one position: the content of an object is its higher structure.

1Kernthese der Fusion

Both papers begin from the same observation, phrased in their own vocabularies. A 28×28 image is not a 2-D picture but a 3-D point cloud: every pixel carries a brightness value, so the image is a spatial grid extruded along an intensity axis. A 0-cell of an ∞-topos is not a bare point but a seed of an unbounded tower: paths (1-morphisms) between points, homotopies (2-morphisms, symmetries of symmetries) between paths, and coherence data above that, “going up forever.” In each case the low-dimensional thing you see is a cross-section; the object’s real content lives in the higher structure it is a slice of.

The fused position is that these are the same idea at different depths. Read at the level of each pixel’s local brightness distribution — the histogram of brightness values that pixel takes across the sample of images — the Foam’s 3-D column and the Topos’s ∞-tower are one object: the tower of levels is the moment expansion of that local distribution (support, mean, variance, skew, …). The Foam is the full local distribution (all moments at once, i.e. depth ∞ at every pixel); the silhouette / max-intensity projection is its depth-0 truncation. And the “Δ aug − raw” volume the Foam isolates is exactly the measurable form of the coherence that the Topos’s functors demand exactly — a transform’s coherence-defect, made visible.

In short: to understand an object, lift it into its full higher-dimensional home and study how its layers cohere. The 3-D foam is a depth-1 window; the ∞-tower is the same window concept carried to the limit. They disagree on finiteness, on statics vs. dynamics, and on exactness vs. approximation — and each disagreement dissolves once the object is read as a sheaf of local distributions with a moment tower.

2Widersprüche zwischen A und B

The two are in genuine tension on three specific points. Each is a real, named claim in the sources, not a manufactured one.

(a) Exhaustible object vs. unbounded structure

A (mnist_foam) treats the object as finite and fully renderable: a bounded 28×28×64 voxel volume you can ray-march, max-intensity-project, and count voxel-by-voxel; the entire distribution is on screen at once, with histograms of voxel count and brightness bin. B (inftytopoi) treats the structure as essentially infinite: the tower of higher morphisms “never stops,” and its own explorer hard-truncates at depth 5 (MAX_DEPTH = 5) precisely because the process cannot terminate. A’s stance: the whole object is visible at once. B’s stance: you can only ever see finite truncations. They make incompatible claims about whether a structure is ever fully exhausted.

(b) Static density vs. relational gluing

A’s central object is a static distribution — voxel counts, a density cloud. “Structure” means the shape and mass of a fixed point set, which you slice, clip, and colour. B’s central objects are processes: local sections gluing into a global sheaf, and functors mapping one layering to another. “Structure” means how parts cohere, not what their density is. A asks “what is the mass distribution?”; B asks “how do local pieces assemble into a whole?”

(c) Approximate, empirical transform vs. exact, formal coherence

A’s augmentation (rotation + shift) is a heuristic, lossy perturbation. The very existence of the Δ aug − raw variant proves the transform is not structure-preserving: it deliberately moves mass, and the app measures how far it departs from the identity. B’s morphisms and functors are exact and structure-preserving by axiom (coherent, associative up to the prescribed higher equivalences). A’s “symmetry” is deliberately broken and empirical; B’s is exact by definition. Opposite stances on whether a transformation preserves structure.

3Auflösung

Each tension resolves once the object is read as a sheaf of local brightness distributions carrying a moment tower.

(a) → Truncation is the bridge, not a limitation

B already works by truncation (depth ≤ 5, “the tower keeps going…”), and A is what a finite truncation of a higher structure looks like. Fused: an ∞-structure is accessed only through finite windows; A is the k = 1 window (2-D → 3-D), B’s graph a k = 5 window. “Exhaustibility” is a limiting ideal, not a mode of access. Every concrete view — A’s volume, B’s depth-5 graph — is a truncation, and that is the fundamental epistemic situation, not a defect to be overcome.

(b) → The density is the stalk; the glue is the assembly

At each pixel (x,y), A stores a local brightness distribution — a function over the brightness bins — which is exactly a sheaf stalk. A’s 2-D field of these local distributions is a presheaf on the pixel grid, and B’s gluing is the mechanism that assembles stalks into the global object. Fused: A’s density field is the lowest layer of a sheaf; B’s gluing explains why the global digit is more than the sum of its pixels — the coherence of how local distributions vary over the 2-D manifold is the higher content. Density (A) and glue (B) are two views of one sheaf: stalks vs. global sections.

(c) → Augmentation is a functor up to defect

B’s functor preserves structure exactly (defect 0). A’s augmentation is a higher-morphism on the foam that is only approximately structure-preserving; the “Δ aug − raw” volume is precisely the measured failure of coherence — the morphism’s defect. Fused: every transformation is a functor; A makes the coherence-defect quantitative and visible (the Δ cloud is the transform’s “curvature”), while B’s exact functors are the zero-defect ideal. The Δ is not a bug but the object’s measurable non-coherence.

4Fusionierter Inhalt

A — the image as a 3-D foam

An image is a 28×28 grid of pixels, each brightness in 0–255, bucketed into bins. The foam is a 3-D histogram with axes x, y ∈ [0,28) and a brightness bin ∈ [0,64). The voxel (x,y,b) holds a count: how many images in the selected volume had a pixel at (x,y) falling in bin b. Three data variants are compared — raw, augmented (rotation + shift), and Δ aug − raw (the augmentation-only residual). Render modes include surface shell, points, ray-marched volume, and max-intensity projection (MIP), which collapses the brightness axis by taking the maximum count along each (x,y) ray — i.e. it projects the 3-D foam down to its 2-D shadow. Counts are shown on a log scale; the digit’s identity lives in the 3-D shape, not the 2-D silhouette.

B — the ∞-topos as a tower

An ∞-topos is a universe of spaces with higher structure. Its levels: 0-cells (objects — points, like open sets), 1-cells (morphisms — paths / continuous maps), 2-cells (2-morphisms — homotopies, “symmetries of symmetries”), and 3/4/5…-cells (higher coherence data), the tower unbounded. Two structural operations: sheaf gluing (local sections that agree on overlaps assemble into a global section) and functoriality (a functor maps one layering to another, commuting with composition). Coherence laws hold; the explorer truncates at depth 5 because the tower never terminates.

The unifying object: a local distribution and its moments

For each pixel (x,y) let h(x,y,b) be the count in brightness bin b (A’s local column). Its moment sequence is a tower:

support/mass  m = Σb h(x,y,b)

mean (1st)  μ1 = (1/m)·Σb b·h(x,y,b)

variance (2nd)  σ² = (1/m)·Σb (b−μ1)²·h(x,y,b)

skew (3rd)  γ = (1/m)·Σb ((b−μ1)/σ)³·h(x,y,b)

This single object plays both roles. A’s foam is the full histogram — all moments at once (the complete local distribution, i.e. depth ∞ at every pixel). B’s tower is the same moments re-indexed as levels: level 0 = support (where it is non-zero → the silhouette), level 1 = mean (the foam surface / brightness axis), level 2 = variance (spread / curvature), level 3 = skew (higher coherence), …. The reason the ∞-limit recovers the whole object is the moment problem: the full sequence of moments determines the distribution. So B’s “going up forever” is exactly A’s “the whole histogram at every pixel.”

Transforms as functors; the defect as coherence

A transform T (augmentation = per-image rotation + shift + shear + noise) acts on each local distribution, hence on the whole moment tower: a functor T*. The exact-functor ideal (B) preserves every moment (defect 0). A’s augmentation is non-exact; its defect δT is the change it induces, and A’s “Δ aug − raw” is δT rendered as a point cloud — the morphism’s curvature / failure of coherence, made visible.

MIP as a projection functor

A’s MIP (max along the brightness axis) is a functor that forgets the brightness axis: it sends the 3-D foam to the 2-D silhouette by an extreme-valued reduction. In B’s language it is a functor between two truncations that discards level 1 and keeps level 0 — a shadow, not a deformation.

Computable recipe (what a later round works from)

Given a sample of images: (1) bin each pixel’s brightness → per-pixel histograms h(x,y,b); (2) compute the moment fields m, μ1, σ², γ; (3) the foam = the histograms themselves; (4) the tower = the moment fields, one stratum per level; (5) the silhouette / MIP = the support / mass (or the axis-maximum); (6) the defect = the difference of two transformed moment fields. That is a complete, self-contained object — flat data, its higher structure, and its coherence-defect.
