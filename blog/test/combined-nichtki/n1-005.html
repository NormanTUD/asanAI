Prime Dual-Coordinate Geometry

Fusion aus: pz3.html ✕ pz5.html

Two interactive instruments, each projecting the prime sequence into a 2-D plane with a different choice of coordinates. Fused, they describe the same object from two complementary axes — and the "ghost structures" one paper hunts for are exactly the correlations the other paper's axis already explains.

Kernthese der Fusion

Both papers plot the primes, but they choose their coordinates differently, and that difference is the whole story. pz3 projects the local rhythm of gaps — the 4-tuple of consecutive gaps (pn, pn+1, pn+2, pn+3) reduced to (log g₁, log g₂, log g₃) — into a warped "latent manifold" and asks whether any cluster is a "ghost structure" that should not exist if primes were truly random. pz5 instead wraps each prime around a modular cylinder at coordinate p mod m (with vertical "gap velocity" gap mod m) and, when m is a primorial, finds the primes collapsing into vertical ribbons — a structure it treats as genuine and law-governed.

The fused position: a prime is a point in a two-axis space, located simultaneously by (a) its residue on the modular cylinder and (b) its gap-rhythm in the log-manifold. These axes are near-orthogonal. The residue axis encodes the arithmetic sieve (why primes avoid small multiples); the gap axis encodes the statistical flow (how gaps distribute once the sieve is removed). A single projection along either axis is blind to what the other shows: the cylinder is blind to gap-correlation, the manifold is blind to residue-constraint.

Unifying claim: primes are not random in the strong sense, but are locally near-random conditional on the sieve — and the real content of prime number theory is the residual: the higher-order correlations (twin primes, constellations, Chebyshev bias) that neither the residue law nor an independent gap law predicts. That residual is what both papers, separately, keep mistaking for either illusion or law.

Widersprüche zwischen A und B

Ontology of randomness

Ghost vs. ribbon

pz3 takes randomness as the null hypothesis: it searches for clusters that "shouldn't exist if primes were truly random," treating visible structure as suspicious — possibly an illusion of the projection. pz5 takes the opposite stance: the ribbons and "attractors" are real deterministic structure that the right coordinate system merely reveals. Same dots, opposite verdict on what a cluster means.

Fundamental unit

Gap-rhythm vs. absolute residue

pz3 plots the sequential gap sequence in log-space — scale-invariant, agnostic to the primes' absolute value. pz5 plots the absolute residue p mod m — value-dependent and modulus-dependent. They use different fundamental coordinates on identical data.

Explanation of structure

Law vs. artifact

pz5 explains its clusters by number theory: primes (other than the small factors of m) are coprime to m, so they occupy only the φ(m) reduced residue classes — a hard constraint producing the ribbons. pz3 leaves its clusters unexplained, posed as open "ghosts." Tension: is a visible cluster a law (B) or a projection artifact (A)?

Physical metaphor

Manifold vs. flow

pz3 pictures primes as a breathing, warped geometric manifold ("the local geometry of randomness," with a warp λ and dimensional rotation). pz5 pictures them as a dynamical flow on a cylinder with "gap velocity," "interference" and "attractors." Two different physical metaphors for the same sequence.

Auflösung

The contradiction dissolves once we read the two instruments as two orthogonal projections of one object, rather than as competing answers to one question. Each resolves a different contradiction:

  1. Ghost vs. ribbon → explained + open. B's ribbons are the arithmetic sieve constraint made visible — genuinely real and genuinely explained (primes are coprime to m). A's "ghosts" are not illusions: they are the residual correlations that survive after conditioning on that sieve. So B is right that structure is real; A is right that, once the residue law is removed, the remaining structure is subtle and worth hunting. The fused answer: most structure is the sieve (B), the interesting remainder is the residual (A).
  2. Gap-rhythm vs. residue → dual coordinates. The two coordinates are not rivals; together they parametrize the space. A prime's full local state is (residue, gap₁, gap₂, …). B reads the first axis, A the rest. A plot blended across both (the λ-slider below) makes this explicit: at λ=0 you see A's manifold, at λ=1 you see B's cylinder, and in between the two structures superpose.
  3. Law vs. artifact → both, at different scales. At the scale of the modulus, structure is a law (sieve). At the scale of the gap distribution within an admissible residue, it is near-random (Cramér-model baseline). "Artifact" is the wrong word for either; the right word is scale.
  4. Manifold vs. flow → one geometry, two pictures. The cylinder is the residue manifold; the warped log-manifold is the gap manifold. Both are legitimate coordinate charts on the prime sequence. The "flow" (B) and the "breathing warp" (A) are the same underlying distributional regularity rendered in two charts.
Fused conclusion

Primes live on a cylinder; their apparent randomness is the shadow of an arithmetic constraint. Conditioned on the sieve, gaps behave near-randomly (A's null hypothesis is the correct baseline), and the scientifically meaningful object is the deviation from the product of the independent residue law and the independent gap law — the residual that is prime number theory's open core.

Fusionierter Inhalt

Shared substrate — the sieve

Both instruments begin with the Sieve of Eratosthenes: mark composites of all primes ≤ √N, and the survivors are the primes p1 < p2 < …. pz5 sizes its sieve by the standard estimate pn ≈ n·log n (it allocates limit×15 as a generous bound). The gap sequence gi = pi+1 − pi is the shared raw signal.

sieve: s[0]=s[1]=0; for i=2..√N: if s[i] then for j=i²..N step i: s[j]=0
gaps: gi = pi+1 − pi    (note gi ≥ 2 for i≥2, and gi≠1,2,3,5,7 for odd pi)

Axis A — the log-gap latent manifold pz3

A forms a feature vector from four consecutive gaps and projects it to a plane with a rotation θ and a nonlinear warp λ:

xlatent = log g₁·cos θ − log g₂·sin θ
ylatent = log g₂·cos(θ+λ) + log g₃·sin(θ+λ)
X = 0.5·W + (xlatent·scale)·tan(0.1λ)    Y = 0.5·H + (ylatent·scale)

Two design choices carry the meaning: log makes the map scale-invariant, so A reads the ratios of gaps (the "rhythm"), not their size; and the fourth gap g₄ is used only as colour — the "future" of the sequence — so each dot is a now-prediction pair. A's explicit task: find clusters ("ghost structures") that a truly random gap process would not produce.

Axis B — the modular-cylinder phase portrait pz5

B wraps each prime onto a cylinder of circumference m:

x = (pi mod m) / m · W     y = 1 − ((gi·k) mod m) / m · H
hue = pi mod 360

The magic values are the primorials m = 6, 30, 210, 2310 (= 2·3·5·7, …). Because every prime > 7 is coprime to such an m, p mod m can only land in the φ(m) reduced residue classes out of m — a fraction φ(m)/m of the cylinder is literally empty. That forbidden-region is the ribbon structure. Nudging m by 0.1 breaks the symmetry and the ribbons "braid." B's readout calls dense regions "statistical attractors in the prime flow."

The unifying model — Cramér + the residual

Both axes meet in Cramér's model: treat primes as an independent random thinning of the integers where n survives with probability 1/log n. This predicts the mean gap ⟨g⟩ ≈ log p and makes gaps look locally Poisson — exactly A's near-randomness baseline. But Cramér is conditioned on the sieve: the survival probability already bakes in B's residue exclusion. So B's ribbons are the conditioning; A's randomness is the thinning inside it.

P(n prime) ≈ ∏p|n 0 · ∏p∤n (1 − 1/p)−1 / log n   (sieved Cramér: B's constraint × A's thinning)
residual = observed gap/constellation statistics − (residue law ⊗ independent-gap law)

The residual is where the genuine, still-open phenomena live: twin primes, prime constellations (the prime-k-tuples conjecture), and Chebyshev bias — the higher-order correlations that are neither pure sieve nor pure random thinning. These are precisely the "clusters that shouldn't exist if primes were truly random" that A hunts, reframed honestly: they are not ghosts, they are the measured failure of the two-factor (residue ⊗ gap) independence model.

Fused coordinate map

Define a blend λ∈[0,1] that interpolates each prime's 2-D position between the two charts, with A's scale-normalized gap features and B's modular features mapped to the same [0,1] box:

A: ax = (log g₁ − mn)/(mx − mn),   ay = (log g₂ − mn)/(mx − mn)
B: bx = (pi mod m)/m,   by = (gi mod m)/m
X(λ) = (1−λ)·ax + λ·bx    Y(λ) = (1−λ)·ay + λ·by

Colouring each point by the next gap g₄ (A's "colour the future") and overlaying a density heat-field makes the residual visible as hot, off-axes blobs — the ghost structures, now placed in a coordinate system that shows both the ribbon law (B, at λ→1) and the gap-rhythm (A, at λ→0) at once.
