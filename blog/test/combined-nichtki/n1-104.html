The Examined Life, Proved Coherent

Fusion aus: philosophy2.html × good.html

Kernthese der Fusion

Both papers are answers to the same 2,600-year-old question — how should one live? philosophy2 (A) opens with Socrates’ “The unexamined life is not worth living” and treats philosophy as “the operating system for your mind”: a plural, first-person toolkit in which you discover your own orientation across moods, dilemmas, and a dozen traditions (Stoic, existentialist, Kantian, virtue, utilitarian, Taoist…). good.html (B) answers the identical question as an engineer: “Nine principles that fit together into one coherent whole,” cast as an axiomatic system (8 axioms, 14 theorems) whose ethical principles form a sheaf over the domains of life — Self, Other, Community — and are proven, in Coq, to glue into a single global section they call good_life.

The single integrated position: the good life is not one rule but a set of local commitments that must hold consistently across every domain of a life. A lives this as experience — many feelings, many situations, many traditions, each a local “what to do,” and the examined life is the habit of checking that they fit. B lives this as proof — the sheaf-condition that local sections agree on their overlaps, so they glue into one global section. Fused: authenticity (A) is what makes the sheaf’s sections honest and specifically yours; consistency/verification (B) is what makes that authenticity coherent rather than arbitrary. You do not so much find a philosophy as you find the set of commitments that both resonate with you (A) and glue without tearing (B). A good life is a global section: locally chosen by the agent, globally non-contradictory.

Widersprüche zwischen A und B

The two papers genuinely part on three stances (not on any shared fact):

1. One system — B

B — good.html

“Nine principles that fit together into one coherent whole.” The final step’s one-liner is “The global section exists: all parts glue into one coherent whole.” There is a single, sufficient system, and it is complete in the covering sense.

1. Many systems — A

A — philosophy2

The Life Simulator states it flatly: “There’s no ‘wrong’ answer. Each response reflects a legitimate philosophical tradition… the goal isn’t to pick the ‘right’ philosophy.” A denies there is one right philosophy; the Builder exists to find yours, not to impose one.

2. Meaning is verified & determinate — B

B — good.html

The good life is a determinate, provable object: good_life a := nonharming a ∧ mindful a ∧ … ∧ growing a, with a machine-checkable consistency theorem (“no internal contradiction”). The structure exists out there and can be checked.

2. Meaning is created, not pre-given — A

A — philosophy2

A’s dominant strand is existentialist: “You believe life has no pre-given meaning — and that’s liberating”; “Man is condemned to be free” (Sartre); Camus’ Absurd: “life has no inherent meaning… rebel… by living fully anyway.” You author the values; there is no fixed essence to discover.

3. You know it is right because it type-checks — B

B — good.html

Correctness is machine-checkable proof: a “Verify” button runs every theorem, a console logs proofs, the claim is “machine-verifiable.” The test is does it glue / does it type-check.

3. You know it is right because it resonates — A

A — philosophy2

Correctness is first-person resonance: the simulator “reveals which tradition guides your instincts,” Mood Rx answers “how you feel right now,” the Builder finds what “matches your worldview.” There is no external correctness condition — only fit with yourself.

Closest real tension, not a hard contradiction: the status of rigor. A’s whole mode is narrative, mood, story and quote — formal verification is simply not on its map. B makes rigor the point (“backed by formal proof, open to critique”). Whether rigor can touch “the good life” at all is where they truly part.

Auflösung

1. Many vs. one — different levels, so they compose. A’s “no single right philosophy” is about which master tradition you identify with (a menu: Stoic, Existentialist, Kantian…). B’s “one coherent whole” is about the small set of local principles that hold in every life regardless of tradition (do no harm, be aware, see the other…). Look at A’s own data: its six “dominant philosophies” (Existentialist, Virtue, Utilitarian, Taoist, Deontologist, Hedonist) all converge on the same concrete steps — and B documents exactly this as “cross-cultural convergence” (the Golden Rule, non-harm, moderation appear in every tradition). Fused conclusion: many legitimate ways to be human (A) all sit on top of one small set of locally-holding, mutually-gluing principles (B). Choose your tradition for the soul (A); live the nine for the hands (B).

2. Created vs. verified meaning — structure is proved, content is lived. B’s sheaf is extensional: the shape any good life must have, and the theorems guarantee that shape is non-contradictory. A’s authenticity is intensional: which sections you actually fill in is yours to author — B even concedes this at Step 7: “Know YOUR limits, not some abstract ideal.” The theorems prove the map is consistent; the agent’s authentic choices decide where to walk it. No contradiction once you see B proves a consistent structure exists while A insists the walking of it is uncoerced.

3. Resonance vs. proof — the examined life is the consistency check. A’s journal prompts (“What belief do I hold that I’ve never questioned?”), the Socratic Method, and the weekly “Existential Audit” are exactly B’s gluing/locality test run by hand: check that your local beliefs agree on their overlaps. B’s Step 3 “Tell the Truth” is defined as the locality condition (“a section is well-defined only if it is locally consistent”) — which is literally A’s Socratic examination. Fused conclusion: A’s introspection is the human, unformalized version of B’s gluing proof. Examination = informal proof; a good life is whatever survives the cross-examination of your own moods.

4. Rigor’s status — B answers A’s Absurd. A’s “Absurd” (no inherent meaning) is met by B’s consistency theorem: there is a non-contradictory way to be; the open question is only whether you live it. A describes the felt absence of meaning; B describes the provable presence of a consistent structure. They are complementary descriptions of the same gap, not rivals.

Fusionierter Inhalt

Shared question

Both are “how to live well” books. A frames it as examination (Socrates) and eudaimonia (flourishing); B frames it as a verified ethical system. Both agree the point is a life that is whole — A’s “operating system for your mind,” B’s “one coherent whole.” Both insist the local (a mood, a domain, a dilemma) and the global (a life, a good life) must be made to fit.

A — the plural, felt toolkit

  • Five branches: Metaphysics, Epistemology, Ethics, Logic, Aesthetics — each a “superpower,” each with a core-wisdom quote and “use it today” moves.
  • Thought experiments: Trolley, Experience Machine (Nozick), Ship of Theseus, What Is It Like to Be a Bat (Nagel), Schrödinger’s Ethics, Veil of Ignorance (Rawls) — each choice reveals a tradition.
  • Paradoxes: Zeno, the Liar (Gödel), Tolerance (Popper), Grandfather, Fermi, the Hard Problem of Consciousness (Chalmers).
  • Life Simulator + Personal Philosophy Builder: dilemmas and 7 questions tally your instinct into a dominant tradition; “there’s no wrong answer.”
  • Mental models & moods: Socratic Method, Occam’s Razor, Amor Fati, Impermanence, Categorical Imperative, Eudaimonia, Eternal Recurrence, The Absurd; and 8 mood prescriptions (anxious/sad/angry/numb/confused/content/motivated/exhausted) each with a quote + a practice.
  • Daily practices & journal: Stoic morning, Seneca evening review, the Socratic pause, the Kantian test, Taoist non-action; 8 journal prompts (localStorage).

B — the axiomatic, verified system

  • 8 axioms (Agents Exist; Awareness Possible; Others Exist; Actions Have Effects; Harm Recognizable; Improvement Possible; Symmetry of Agents; Measure Bounds Length), each with a “how this could be wrong” + historical test.
  • 9 steps, each with everyday practice, an integrated critique, philosophical sources, a sheaf-theoretic role, and a Coq proof: 0 Do No Harm (zero section) · 1 Be Aware (section on Self) · 2 See the Other (functor Self→Other) · 3 Tell the Truth (locality condition) · 4 Own It (covariant functor) · 5 Be Fair (natural transformation) · 6 Help (non-trivial section) · 7 Know Your Measure (compactness) · 8 Grow (path in HoTT) · 9 That Is All (the global section).
  • Sheaf reading: open sets = domains of life Self, Other, Community; sections = ethical principles; the gluing theorem says local sections that agree on all overlaps glue into the global section good_life. A compatibility matrix marks pairs that share an open set (green) vs. bridge-only (amber). A dependency tower: Logic → Math (type theory/ZFC) → Physics → Biology → Sociology → Ethics.

The fused claim, in one line: the good life is a global section of a sheaf whose open sets are your moods and whose local sections are the principles you authentically endorse — and it exists exactly when your local choices agree on every overlap.
