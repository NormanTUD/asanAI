01 · The ceiling

More heads, shorter keys, worse matching

At a fixed parameter budget, every extra head makes each key and value vector shorter. Past a point, the query–key dot product is too low-dimensional to say anything useful. The plot shows what happens.

Solid line: talking-heads. Dashed line: standard multi-head. Lower perplexity is better; higher accuracy is better.

The tell: standard multi-head attention gets worse when you go from 24 to 48 heads and shrink dk from 32 to 16. Talking-heads keeps improving. The fix is not more heads; it is letting the heads exchange information.

02 · The mechanism

Two projections across the head dimension

Standard heads are parallel and silent. Talking-heads inserts a learned linear map over the head axis immediately before the softmax, and a second one immediately after. Each head's attention can now depend on all the other heads' logits and weights.

03 · The price

Almost free, if your hardware likes small matrices

The extra parameters are two h×h matrices. The extra compute is a small matmul over the head axis. At 12 heads that is 288 parameters and roughly 5% more multiplies per attention layer.

12 heads. Two 12×12 matrices add 288 parameters to a layer with 2.36 million. That is about 0.012%. The layer's key/value dimensions are unchanged.

The catch

The paper is candid about the cost: modern accelerators are tuned for large matrix multiplies, and these head-sized matmuls can be inefficient in practice. The authors flag better small-matmul hardware, or reducing the number of memory positions, as future work.

So why do it? The quality gain is real and the parameter cost is tiny. The compute grows with h: roughly +5% at 12 heads, but about +75% at 48 heads on T5, because both mixing matrices are h×h.

04 · The ablation

You need both ends

Applying only the logits projection, or only the weights projection, recovers part of the gain. Doing both is clearly better. T5 at 24 heads, lower ln PPL is better.

Click a bar. The logits projection lets heads share their raw matching scores; the weights projection lets them share the normalized attention. They are complementary.

05 · Takeaway

What talking-heads changes

The problem

Multi-head attention scales by splitting the same budget into more, smaller heads. Beyond a point, each head's keys are too short for a sharp query–key match, and quality degrades.

The move

Insert one learned linear map over the head dimension before the softmax and one after. Heads stop being independent ensembles and start sharing a representation.

The evidence

On T5, ALBERT and BERT, talking-heads beats multi-head at every head count tested, and keeps improving where multi-head turns down. Both projections matter.

The caveat

The extra computation is small but shaped awkwardly for accelerators. The paper treats efficient small-dimension matmul as open work, not as a solved problem.
