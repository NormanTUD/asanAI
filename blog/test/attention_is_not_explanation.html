A heatmap is not a reason.

Attention weights look like an explanation: the model “looks at” these words, so these words must be why. Two tests show that this story usually fails, even when the prediction is correct.

02 · Test one

Does attention agree with feature importance?

If attention explains a prediction, it should roughly agree with other measures of which tokens matter: input gradients and leave-one-out (erase a word and see how much the output moves). Kendall's τ is the agreement: 0 means none, 1 means perfect.

BiLSTM encoder simple average encoder dashed line = τ 0.5

Read the gap. For the BiLSTM, attention agrees only weakly with gradient importance. For a simple feed-forward embedding encoder, attention is much closer to a direct linear weighting, and the agreement jumps. The same pattern holds for leave-one-out importance. The paper's explanation: in a recurrent encoder the hidden state already mixes all words, so attention over hidden states is not attention over the input words.

03 · The verdict

What fails, and what that does not mean

✕  not faithful

Attention does not track importance

Across SST, IMDB, ADR tweets, 20 Newsgroups, AG News, QA and NLI, attention weights correlate only weakly and inconsistently with gradients and leave-one-out scores. The correlation is not reliable enough to read a reason off the heatmap.

✕  not unique

Different attention, same output

The authors find adversarial attention distributions that are very far from the original (high JSD) yet leave the prediction within ε. If several incompatible heatmaps “explain” the same output equally well, the heatmap does not single out a cause.

The careful version: the paper argues that attention weights are not faithful explanations, not that attention is useless. The mechanism still computes a useful weighted summary, and later work has argued about how far the negative result generalizes. The practical lesson is narrower and sharper: do not present a heatmap as the reason for a prediction without an independent check.

04 · Takeaway

The three sentences to remember

Correlation is weak

Attention and gradient importance often disagree. The gap is largest exactly where the encoder is complex and recurrent.

Counterfactuals are cheap

You can often find a very different attention distribution that leaves the prediction almost unchanged. A map that is not unique is not an explanation.

Structure matters

Simple feed-forward encoders behave better than BiLSTMs. The interpretability of attention depends on the architecture around it.

Test before you trust

Faithfulness is an empirical property. Check it with perturbations and independent importance measures; do not infer it from the shape of the heatmap.
