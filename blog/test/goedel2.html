🔢 Gödel Numbering: Turning Math into Numbers

Gödel's first brilliant trick: encode every symbol, formula, and proof as a unique natural number. This lets mathematics talk about itself.

Step 2: Encode a sequence using prime factorization

A sequence of symbols with codes n₁, n₂, n₃, ... becomes:

2n₁ × 3n₂ × 5n₃ × 7n₄ × ...

Since prime factorization is unique, we can always decode the original sequence!

⚡ The Proof — Step by Step

Let's walk through Gödel's argument. Click "Next Step" to reveal each part.

1 Setup: Consider system P (Peano arithmetic + logic of PM). Every formula and proof gets a unique Gödel number.
2 Define "Provable": Show that "x is a proof of y" (written xBy) is a recursive relation on numbers, hence expressible in P.
3 Class sign R(n): List all formulas with one free variable. R(n) is the n-th such formula. [R(n); k] means "substitute k into R(n)".
4 Define set K: n ∈ K ⟺ ¬Bew[R(n); n] — i.e., n belongs to K iff the formula you get by plugging n into R(n) is not provable.
5 K is expressible! Since all its ingredients are expressible in P, K corresponds to some class sign S. So S = R(q) for some specific number q.
6 The magic formula: Consider [R(q); q]. This formula says "q ∈ K" — which means "[R(q); q] is not provable". It talks about itself!
7 Case A: If [R(q); q] IS provable → then it's true → so it's NOT provable. Contradiction! 💥
8 Case B: If ¬[R(q); q] is provable → then q ∈ K holds → so [R(q); q] IS provable. Both provable = contradiction! 💥
9 Conclusion: [R(q); q] is undecidable — neither it nor its negation can be proven. Yet, from the outside, we see it's true!

💥 The Two Incompleteness Theorems

🥇 First Incompleteness Theorem (Satz VI)

For every ω-consistent recursive class κ of formulas, there exist recursive class signs r such that neither v Gen r nor Neg(v Gen r) belongs to Flg(κ).

In plain English: Any sufficiently powerful, consistent formal system contains true statements that cannot be proven within the system.

🥈 Second Incompleteness Theorem (Satz XI)

The formula that expresses "κ is consistent" is not κ-provable. In particular, the consistency of P cannot be proven within P itself (assuming P is consistent).

In plain English: No consistent system can prove its own consistency.

🎯 Theorem VII: Every recursive relation is arithmetic

Any question about provability can be reframed as a pure question about integers involving +, ×, and quantifiers.

🎯 Theorem X: Undecidability in first-order logic

The decision problem (Entscheidungsproblem) for first-order predicate logic is unsolvable.

🌌 Why It Matters

💔 The Death of Hilbert's Program

Hilbert dreamed of proving mathematics both complete (every truth provable) and consistent (no contradictions) using finite methods. Gödel showed this is impossible for any system rich enough to include arithmetic.

🌊 Ripple Effects

  • Turing (1936): Building on Gödel, proved the Halting Problem is undecidable → foundations of computer science
  • Church: Independently proved undecidability of first-order logic
  • Cohen (1963): Continuum Hypothesis independent of ZFC — a concrete Gödelian statement
  • Philosophy of mind: Penrose & others argue this shows minds transcend algorithms (controversial!)
  • AI & consciousness: Ongoing debates about what "understanding" means
"Gödel's theorem is the most signed, sealed, and delivered bad news for the ambitions of formalism." — Douglas Hofstadter

🎨 The Beautiful Paradox

Gödel used pure logic and arithmetic to prove that logic and arithmetic have fundamental limits. He didn't destroy mathematics — he revealed its infinite depth. There will always be new truths, new theorems, new frontiers.

Mathematics is not a closed book — it's an open, endless adventure. 🚀
