Are sixteen heads really better than one?

Multi-head attention trains many parallel attention heads. At test time, most of them are dead weight. This is the ablation that made Transformers look surprisingly redundant.

The exception matters. Encoder–decoder attention is the one component that genuinely needs its heads: reducing its last layer to one head costs 13.56 BLEU. For BERT, by contrast, every single-head reduction in the paper is statistically insignificant.

05 · Takeaway

What the paper actually claims

Redundancy is real

In trained models, many heads can be removed with no significant loss. Some layers can be reduced to a single head. Multi-head attention is not always using its extra expressive power.

But not everywhere

Encoder–decoder attention is fragile: keep only one head in its last layer and you lose 13.56 BLEU. The network cannot be collapsed to a single-head model everywhere without retraining.

Pruning buys speed

Removing 20% (WMT) or 40% (BERT) of heads is free. At 50% pruning, BERT gets up to 17.5% faster at large batch sizes. The gain vanishes at batch 1.

It is a training story

Head importance is not fixed from initialization. It differentiates as training proceeds: early epochs are egalitarian, later epochs concentrate the load.
