Sheaves, Persistence, and the Born–Persist–Die Machine

Fusion aus: nn_as_sheaf_2.html ✗ persistent_topology.html

Kernthese der Fusion

These two papers look at different objects — a neural-network function (A) and the shape of a point cloud (B) — but both are built on the same template: take a domain, decompose it by a parameter into local pieces, let each piece be born, let it persist over a range of that parameter, let it die (killed by a restriction/boundary map or absorbed into a larger whole), and then assemble the survivors into a global object.

In A the pieces are the ReLU local sections; the domain ℝ is covered by the intervals on which each section is active, the restriction map is the ReLU itself (it kills a section on one side of its bend), and the global section is the glued — in fact the additive — sum. In B the pieces are homology classes; the data is filtered by radius ε, a connected component is born at ε = 0 and dies when it merges, a loop is born when a cycle closes and dies when a triangle fills it (i.e. when it becomes a boundary). The single integrated position: a network and a persistence module are two realizations of one "born–persist–die–glue" machine, and the part that is genuinely meaningful in both cases is exactly the part that persists — the local pieces long-lived enough to survive into the global object.

Widersprüche zwischen A und B

The two do not directly contradict (different subject matter), but they take opposing stances on three specific points:

  • Additive gluing (A) vs. noise-filtering selection (B). A says the global section is the coherent sum of all local sections, ŷ = Σ αₒ·ReLU(wₒx+bₒ) + β; even the ReLU-"killed" half of a section is kept in the cover as the zero section, and no piece is ever discarded — every neuron contributes αₒhₒ. B says the whole point is to separate signal from noise: "Short bars = noise. Long bars = real structure." Most born features are explicitly thrown away; only the long-lived (persistent) ones count. A keeps everything; B selects.
  • Sharp / fragile local exactness (A) vs. coarse / stable global invariants (B). A celebrates exact local structure — each bend is the sharp coordinate x*ₒ = −bₒ/wₒ and every equation is shown "with real values"; a small change in wₒ or bₒ moves the bend continuously, and A claims no robustness guarantee. B makes the Stability Theorem (Cohen–Steiner, Edelsbrunner, Harer 2007) its headline: small input perturbations produce only small (bottleneck-distance) changes in the diagram. A is exact-but-fragile at the local level; B is deliberately insensitive to local detail by design.
  • Opposite direction of the assembly maps: contravariant restriction (A) vs. covariant inclusion (B). A is a contravariant system — a sheaf's maps are restriction maps that go from a larger open set to a smaller one (F(U) → F(V) for U ⊃ V), and here they kill the section (ReLU → 0). Assembly runs downward / by restriction. B is a covariant system — a persistence module's inclusion / structure maps go from small scale to large scale (K_ε → K_ε′, ε ≤ ε′) and preserve features. Assembly runs upward / by inclusion. The two assemble their global object along opposite arrows.

Auflösung

  • Selection is gluing with a persistence threshold. Every local piece carries a lifetime — in A the length of the x-interval over which a section is nonzero; in B the ε-interval over which a feature is nontrivial. "Signal vs. noise" then just means "long-lived vs. short-lived." A's additive gluing is the zero-threshold limit of B's selection: in A every section is structural by construction (the network was built to have exactly these pieces, none degenerate), so "keep all" already equals "keep the persistent." In B, unstructured data spawns many immediately-dying (near-diagonal) features, so selection drops them. Fused conclusion: the sheaf's global section and the persistence module's persistent feature are the same notion — a local structure that survives long enough to matter; gluing (A) is superposition of all local pieces, which reduces to selection (B) once the pieces are guaranteed persistent.
  • Exactness and stability are two resolutions of one object. A gives the fine, parameter-exact description (the network is its piecewise formula); B gives the coarse, perturbation-stable description (the topological signature, invariant under small changes). Fused conclusion: stability is what you recover by forgetting the exact weights and keeping only persistence. A's individual bend points are fragile, but the set of persistence bars (which features survive, roughly where) is stable in exactly the bottleneck sense B formalizes — so the exact piecewise function (A) is a point in the space of networks, and the persistence of its sublevel/superlevel sets (B) is a stable, low-dimensional, robust fingerprint of that network.
  • Restriction and inclusion are dual arrows of one assembly. The two are not contradictory but dual: a sheaf (contravariant, restriction maps) and a persistence module (covariant, inclusion maps) are opposite functors on dual posets (opens ordered by ⊃ vs. scales ordered by ≤). Both assemble a global object from a compatible family of local ones by a universal construction — the sheaf's gluing axiom (compatible local sections → a unique global section) corresponds to the persistence module's direct limit / colimit. Fused conclusion: the "global" in both theories is a colimit of a local system — a colimit over a spatial cover in A, over a scale filtration in B. Same mechanism, two directions, two parameters (position x vs. scale ε).

Fusionierter Inhalt

A — the network as a sheaf

The object is the simplest non-trivial network, one hidden layer with N ReLU neurons:

ŷ(x) = Σ αₒ·ReLU(wₒx+bₒ) + β, ReLU(u)=max(0,u)

Each neuron is a local section: a line wₒx + bₒ that ReLU bends at its zero crossing x*ₒ = −bₒ/wₒ and keeps only on the active side (exactly zero on the other). The sheaf dictionary of A:

Stance: the global output is a sum of local pieces — constructive, additive, local-to-global.

B — persistent homology as shape-finding

The object is a point cloud. Build the Vietoris–Rips filtration K_ε by growing balls of radius ε: an edge appears when two balls meet, a triangle when three mutually meet. Track three feature types across ε — H₀ connected components, H₁ loops, H₂ voids — recording each feature's birth ε and death ε. The result is a barcode (one bar per feature) or a persistence diagram (points at (birth, death)); points far from the diagonal are long-lived = signal, points near the diagonal are noise.

Algorithms: H₀ via union-find / Kruskal (a component dies when an edge merges two clusters); H₁ via boundary-matrix reduction over ℤ/2 (a cycle-creating edge is a birth, a triangle kills the cycle it bounds). The headline is the Stability Theorem: the bottleneck distance between diagrams is bounded by the perturbation of the input — hence robustness.

The unified template: born – persist – die – glue

Both decompose a domain by a parameter and each piece dies by a boundary/restriction operation: in A a local section is cut by the restriction map (ReLU) at its bend; in B a loop dies precisely when it becomes a boundary (a triangle fills it, ∂(triangle) = loop ⇒ the loop is trivial in H₁), and a component dies when an edge glues two clusters. The "global" object in both is a colimit of a local system: sheaf gluing over a spatial cover (A) ↔ persistence direct-limit over a scale filtration (B). The two parameters are orthogonal — A's position x and B's scale ε — so together they form a (position, scale) plane; A is the spatial slice, B the scale slice.

The two "death" operations are algebraically the same kind of map:

  • Sheaf restriction: s → ρ(s) = 0 on the side U⁻ where the section is killed.
  • Boundary/filling: a loop is killed exactly when it is the boundary of a 2-simplex, i.e. ∂(triangle) = loop, so the loop is trivial in H₁.

The concrete fused construction (implemented in the viz)

Apply B's 1-D sublevel-set persistence to A's output function:

X_t = { x : ŷ(x) ≤ t }

Each local minimum of ŷ births a connected component of X_t; each local maximum kills the shallower of the two components it joins (the deeper one survives). The resulting barcode is a stable topological fingerprint of the network: a small perturbation of the weights moves the exact bend points (A's fragility) but only slightly shifts the persistence bars (B's stability, mirroring the bottleneck bound d_B(D, D′) ≤ δ for input perturbation ≤ δ). This is the fusion made executable: the network's local-to-global gluing (A) read through the born–persist–die barcode (B).
