One Attention Field, Two Readouts

Fusion aus: qkv.html ✕ simplicial_complex_attention5.html

Kernthese der Fusion

Both papers describe the same primitive and disagree only about what to read off it. The primitive is a token-to-token similarity field: each token gets an embedding, and the "score" between two tokens is a dot product of (linearly transformed) embeddings, sharpened by a softmax temperature. A (qkv.html) reads this field as a directed, continuous convex combination of value vectors — a chosen query's output is o = Σj αj vj with αj = softmax(q·kj/√d), so every token always contributes a positive share that sums to one. B (simplicial_complex_attention5.html) reads the very same field as a combinatorial object: it symmetrizes the attention by mutual agreement, cuts it with a threshold ε, and treats the surviving pairs as the 1-skeleton of a simplicial complex (triangles where three tokens are mutually connected), then extracts Betti numbers.

The fused position: attention is a single similarity field, and "reading values" (A) and "reading topology" (B) are two projections of one operation. A's soft row-integral against the value vectors is the content-level, 0-dimensional view; B's thresholded, symmetrized, hierarchically-filled version is the structural/topological view. Nothing in either view is new information — both are functions of the one field s(i,j). The projection matrices WQ/WK/WV (A's "twist" knobs) and the softmax temperature shape what the field looks like, while ε (B) sets where you cut the field to expose discrete structure.

Widersprüche zwischen A und B

  • A says the output is a convex combination: o = Σj αj vj with αj > 0 for every j and Σαj = 1, "the output is pulled mostly toward X's V vector (78% weight)". It is smooth, always fully connected, with no threshold and no discrete object. B says connectivity is binary and thresholded: an edge exists iff mutual attention > ε, and the object is a simplicial complex with a definite 1-skeleton and filled triangles. So A's readout is a smooth vector; B's readout is a combinatorial complex. In A nothing is ever "disconnected"; in B the entire point is which pairs/triples are connected.
  • A is directed: one selected query token attends to all the keys; the score q·kj is asymmetric in role (one query, many keys) and the value read flows query→keys→values. B symmetrizes: the edge weight is min(αij, αji) — an undirected edge appears only where attention is mutual (both directions agree above ε). So A's unit is a directed read (which one token reads), B's unit is an undirected agreement (where two tokens attend to each other).
  • A makes the learned projections the mechanism: "twist the WQ/WK knobs to change what counts as relevant; twist WV to change what gets read" — without the linear maps there is no matching. B deliberately strips them out: "single-head, no learned weights for clarity — the metric is just cosine-like similarity" on fixed, interpretable 3D axes (gender / being / power). So A says W is load-bearing; B shows the same "attention" running on raw dot/cosine with W = identity.

Auflösung

  1. Soft weights are the edge weights. A's convex combination and B's skeleton read the same adjacency field S[i][j] two ways: A integrates the soft row (Σ αj vj); B applies a hard threshold (a hard-sigmoid / quantization) to expose the 1-skeleton, then fills triangles. ε is a decision boundary on one continuous field: as ε→0 the complex becomes the complete graph (all soft edges visible); as ε→∞ it empties. The convex blend is the "relaxed" version of the same connectivity, and the readout is a single dial (ε / temperature) along the soft↔discrete axis.
  2. Directionality and symmetry are two roles of one field, not a contradiction. The directed attention αij (query i reads key j) is the primitive. B's min(αij, αji) is a symmetrization that asks "do they attend to each other?" — extracting the undirected mutual-agreement substructure from the directed field. In the fused model the directed field carries content flow (the value read, A) while its symmetrized-and-thresholded part carries topology (the skeleton, B). The query→key→value pipeline and the mutual edge are the directed read and the symmetric skeleton of one matrix.
  3. B is the identity-projection limit of A. A computes s(i,j) = (WQxi)·(WKxj); B computes xi·xj — exactly A with WQ=WK=WV=I (B literally uses identity weights). So B is not a rival mechanism; it is A evaluated at W=I on a fixed semantic basis. Keeping W as a free dial, the twist knobs (A) rotate the field and thereby reshape which triples close into triangles — i.e. W controls topology, not just readout — so the Betti numbers become a functional of (W, temperature, ε).

Fusionierter Inhalt

The shared primitive. Let xi ∈ ℝd be the embedding of token i, with Q=XWQ, K=XWK, V=XWV. The raw score is rij = qi·kj = (WQxi)·(WKxj), scaled by √d; with temperature T (A's "focus", the inverse of B's "sharpness") the attention row is

αij = softmaxj( rij / √d / T )  =   exp(rij/√d/T) / Σℓ exp(riℓ/√d/T)

Readout 1 — content (A): the query's output is a convex combination of the value vectors,

oi = Σj αij vj ,   αij ≥ 0,  Σj αij = 1

which is exactly the "matching (Q·K) → reading (V)" picture: the output is pulled toward the value of the key the query aligns with most (smallest angle, largest weight).

Readout 2 — structure (B): symmetrize, threshold, fill:

wij = min(αij, αji)  ·  edge ⇔ wij > ε  ·  triangle ⇔ all three w of a triple > ε

The surviving edges are the 1-skeleton of a simplicial complex K = (V, E, F); a triangle (2-simplex) is filled when three tokens are mutually connected — B's "these words are thought about together". Topology is then summarized by Betti numbers: β0 = connected components (union-find over E), and B's β1 ≈ |E| − (n − β0) − |F| (a cyclomatic-style approximation, not a full homology computation).

The dials and their limits. W reshapes the field (rotating it re-pairs the tokens); T trades peakiness (T→0 locks the query onto its single best key — A's "smallest angle"; T→∞ flattens to the uniform average of all values); ε trades softness for discreteness (ε→0 complete graph, ε→∞ empty complex). Locality unifies both claims: A's "one token pulls mostly toward its best-matching key" and B's "meaning focuses locally" are the same low-temperature, high-ε limit of the field, where a soft row collapses to one dominant weight and the complex collapses to a sparse skeleton.

Caveats carried faithfully. A uses 2D rotations of hand-picked embeddings (not learned full-rank W) and a single query/head; B uses 3D rule-based lexicon embeddings, identity weights, and an approximate β1. The fusion is conceptual: the visualization below keeps 2D hand-picked embeddings, adds the W twist knobs (A), the temperature T, the threshold ε, and the Betti readout (B) so both readouts run off one field.
