## How many slices can a net actually carve? The folding intuition above has a clean theorem behind it. A rectifier (ReLU) network with a total of $N$ neurons can split its input space into **at most $2^N$ linear regions** — one per pattern of which neurons are active \cite[Montúfar et al., 2014 — max regions ≤ 2^N (Prop. 3); constructive depth bound (Thm. 4)]{montufar2014regions}. Depth is what turns this into an explosion: the number a *deep* net can actually **guarantee** grows roughly like $\left(\frac{m}{n_0}\right)^{(L-1)\,n_0}\,m^{\,n_0}$ — exponential in depth $L$, not merely in size. Pick a known model (or type in your own $L$, $m$, $n_0$) and compare three readouts for the *same* neuron budget: the worst-case ceiling, what a deep net can provably reach, and what a flat net would get.

⚠️ Why the LLM numbers are a loose analogy, not a literal bound. All of this is exact only for pure ReLU (piecewise-linear) nets. Modern LLMs differ, so read those presets as a sense of scale: their feed-forward block uses GELU (GPT) or SwiGLU / SiLU (LLaMA) — smooth, not piecewise-linear — so there are no crisp "linear regions" to count at all; and they interleave attention (softmax) and normalization, which are not dense ReLU blocks either. The exponential-in-depth intuition still carries over; the exact slice count does not.
