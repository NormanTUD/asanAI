The overview

Why a calendar?

Most of us meet AI the same way: a chatbot answers a question, a headline warns about the future, a colleague says "you have to try this." The result is a strange mix of familiarity and confusion. We use the tools every day, but few of us can explain what is actually happening inside them.

This page is an attempt to fix that. It is a 31-day reading calendar for curious adults — not a course for engineers. Each day opens one idea, points you to the clearest interactive lesson on the site, and tells you what you should be able to explain afterward. The first days are gentle. The middle is the technical core. The last days zoom out.

You do not need a technical background or a GPU. The lessons explain each idea in plain language. You can read one door a day, or use the page as a map and jump to whatever you need.

What you will be able to explain

  • What a language model is actually doing when it answers
  • Why "predict the next word" can look like understanding
  • How a network learns from its mistakes
  • What a transformer is, in plain language
  • Why models hallucinate, and what retrieval does
  • Where the money, the power, and the open questions are

Who it is for

  • Teachers who want a clear explanation
  • Journalists who need the vocabulary
  • Managers who have to make decisions about AI
  • Students who want the shape of the field
  • Parents who want to answer "what is ChatGPT?"
  • Anyone tired of both hype and condescension

What it is not

It is not a coding bootcamp. It is not a guide to prompt tricks. It is not a promise that AI will solve everything, or a warning that it will end everything. It is a map of the ideas, with the hype turned down.

How to use it

Read one door a day. If a day feels heavy, read the first link and stop. Each card also has a "short on time" pick. The lessons are interactive: you can play with a tokenizer, train a tiny network, change a temperature, and watch a model's attention move. You do not need to install anything.

December is a good excuse for a daily habit, but the calendar works in any month. The numbers are a promise of sequence, not a deadline. Start today.

The goal is not to turn you into a machine-learning engineer. The goal is to make you a confident reader of the field: able to tell a real capability from a demo, a benchmark from a marketing claim, and a genuinely new idea from a very old argument.
