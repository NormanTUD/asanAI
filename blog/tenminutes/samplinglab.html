The distribution is not the answer.

A language model ends with a probability for every token. Decoding is the rule that picks one. Change the rule and the same model sounds focused, repetitive, or wild.

01 Temperature and greedy

Sharp or flat.

Temperature rescales the logits before softmax. Near zero, the most likely token dominates and decoding becomes greedy and deterministic. Above one, the distribution flattens and the model takes more risks.

Greedy decoding is not 'the truth'; it is just the highest-probability continuation. It can be repetitive and can miss a better sentence that starts with a less likely token.

02 Filters and trade-offs

No single best setting.

Top-k keeps only the k most likely tokens. Top-p keeps the smallest set whose probabilities sum to p. Min-p uses a threshold relative to the top token. Repetition penalties discourage loops.

There is no single best setting. Code and facts often want low temperature; brainstorming wants more diversity. The right choice depends on the task, and every choice is a trade-off between coherence and surprise.
