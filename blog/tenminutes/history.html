A very
long fuse.

Modern AI did not appear in 2022. It grew out of counting bones, geared astronomical models, formal logic, a failed neural network, two winters, and a crowd of contributors who are usually left out of the story. This is the lineage, not the legend.

01 The long fuse

History is convergence, not a conveyor belt.

The history of AI is not a straight line leading inevitably to the large language model. It is a convergence of ideas from unrelated fields that were never intended to meet. Statistical methods developed by astronomers to map the stars now weigh the next word in a sequence. GPU architectures designed for video games provide the parallel computation that neural networks need.

The deeper history is a history of abstraction: human effort drawn away from the direct task and handed to a mechanism. It begins roughly 3.3 million years ago with the first stone tools, continues through the wheel and Heron of Alexandria’s programmable cart, and crosses from the physical to the cognitive when thought itself becomes a formal calculus a machine can run.

We can solve any problem by introducing an extra level of indirection. David Wheeler, quoted in the full chapter
The aha

The machine is the ultimate indirection.

The wheel abstracts movement; the computer abstracts thought. Both remove the human from the raw labour while keeping the human in charge of the mechanism.

03 Three currents

Logic, machines, and minds.

Three currents feed the modern model. Formal thought — place value, zero, syllogisms, Pāṇini’s grammar, Boole’s algebra — supplies the symbol manipulation. Machines — gears, relays, vacuum tubes, transistors, GPUs — supply the physical substrate. Minds — neurons, cybernetics, learning, reward — supply the metaphor and, eventually, the method.

None of them was sufficient alone. The transformer is what happens when a statistical learning idea meets a parallel piece of hardware and a formal language for representing meaning as vectors.

04 What the history teaches

No inevitability, no lone genius.

The standard story is a parade of famous names and sudden breakthroughs. The actual history is slower and more collective. Counting devices appear independently in southern Africa, central Europe, and central Africa. The first neural network paper lands in a biophysics journal, not a computer science one. The perceptron is announced by the U.S. Navy, criticised, and survives in the margins until the hardware and the data catch up.

There were winters: periods when the promises outran the results and the money left. There were also quiet accumulations — the stored-program idea, the transistor, the GPU, the dataset, the optimizer — without which the same algorithm would be a curiosity. The transformer did not end history; it condensed a very long fuse.

The aha

The past was not trying to become ChatGPT.

The people who built tally bones, gears, logic, and perceptrons were solving their own problems. The convergence is real, but it is retrospective — which is exactly why it is worth studying.

Explain it back

Why is the history of AI better described as convergence than as a straight line? Name two of the currents.

Compare with a model answer

Because the modern model is where several independent traditions meet: formal thought (place value, logic, grammar), machines (gears, relays, GPUs), and minds (neurons, feedback, learning). Each was solving its own problems. The fusion was retrospective, and it included winters and detours — not an inevitable march toward ChatGPT.
