Turn the network around.

Normally we adjust the weights to fit an image. Inceptionism flips the direction: freeze the weights and adjust the image until it becomes what the network recognizes.

01 The idea

The reverse direction.

A classifier is trained to map an image to a label. If you freeze its weights and run gradient ascent on a class score, the pixels change instead of the parameters. Start from noise, ask for 'banana', and the noise slowly organizes into banana-like shapes.

The result looks surreal because the network is not drawing a photograph. It is projecting its learned features back into pixel space. Low layers produce edges and strokes; higher layers assemble whole objects.

02 What the dreams reveal

A window into learned features.

Inceptionism is a simple interpretability tool. It shows that the network's knowledge is distributed across layers and can be visualized by optimizing the input. It also explains pareidolia: like a child seeing animals in clouds, the network finds its templates in noise.

The images are not evidence that the model is conscious or dreaming in the human sense. They are a picture of what the weights have learned to respond to.
