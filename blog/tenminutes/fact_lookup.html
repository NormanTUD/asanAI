A fact is a pattern, not a row.

The model has no table called 'countries'. 'Paris' emerges from the interaction of weights, attention, and a residual stream. The picture is partial, and the honesty matters.

01 A forward pass, not a lookup

The computation produces the fact.

The prompt becomes tokens, then vectors. Each transformer block reads from a residual stream, lets attention route information between positions, and lets the MLP write an update back into the stream. The final vector is scored against the vocabulary.

The residual stream is a shared scratchpad. Attention can move the meaning of 'France' to the final position; the MLP can act like a learned key-value memory. The fact is distributed across many weights, not stored in one place.

02 What we know and what we do not

Partial maps, honest limits.

Mechanistic interpretability has found circuits and directions that correspond to real behaviour, and methods like ROME can edit a fact by changing specific weights. But the full story is not known, even by the people who build the models.

The practical lesson is not that the model has a tidy database. It is that knowledge is entangled with computation, which is why a model can be confident and wrong, and why editing one fact can have side effects.
