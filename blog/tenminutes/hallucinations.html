Plausible is not the same as true.

A language model is a pattern completion engine, not a database. That is the source of its fluency and the source of its most dangerous failure.

01 The idea

It completes patterns.

The model does not look up facts in a table. It has learned, from a very large amount of text, which continuations usually follow which contexts. Ask for a medical study and it knows what a study title looks like; it can produce a convincing one that does not exist.

This is not lying in the human sense. The model has no intention to deceive and no internal fact-checker. It is doing the only thing it was trained to do: assigning probability to the next token.

02 Why it sounds confident

Confidence is not evidence.

Confidence is a property of the text, not of the model's knowledge. A fluent answer with a plausible citation can be wrong in every detail. Temperature makes this worse: a higher temperature flattens the distribution and can push the model toward less likely, more inventive continuations.

Use the model as a draft, a tutor, or a search aid — not as an oracle. Verify names, numbers, quotes, and citations. Give it documents with retrieval, ask it to show its sources, and keep a human in the loop for anything that matters.
